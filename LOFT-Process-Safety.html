1.0 Introduction and Preparation

1.1 Introduction

This Assessor Guide provides the questions and model answers across ten Process Safety categories for the Global Process Safety Modules. The answers to these questions should be the same for all Upstream Oil and Gas Business Units globally. It also contains the Asset Specific questions. It is an expectation that each Business Unit develop their own Asset Specific Assessor Guide from this template with the asset/facility specific answers to each of these questions. This guide should be utilized by both the candidate, in preparing for their face to face interview, and by the assessment teams, to ensure a consistency in assessment across all Business Units. This Guide is referred to as Appendix B under the LOFT Framework UBP.

This Combined Assessor Guide should be used for a specified position to conduct a complete assessment of Process Safety competency. This guide should be utilized by both the candidate, in preparing for their face to face interview, and by the assessment teams, to ensure a consistency in assessment across all Business Units. This Guide is referred to as Appendix B under the LOFT Framework UBP.

The assessment process can be undertaken once the candidate has completed their task book and is ready to demonstrate their competency. It is expected that this is conducted within 90 days of assuming position. Risk Approval Authority for each position cannot be granted until the competency in the Process Safety Modules is demonstrated.

1.2 Purpose of Leadership Assessor Guide

  • To provide a Global and Asset Specific assessment tool for new leaders to demonstrate competency in managing process safety and risk.
  • Provide consistency in assessment of personnel in LOFT Process Safety positions across Upstream Oil and Gas.
  • Provide a single point of resource material for Assessment Teams and Candidates.

1.3 Supervisor’s Role

  1. Review the Leadership Process Safety Competency Demonstration Assessor Guide / expectations with the candidate.
  2. Based on the individual’s previous assignments/work experiences assess the current “gap” for each section.
  3. Identify any extra developmental activities that would assist the candidate to improve their depth of understanding.
  4. Based on current gaps and recommended developmental activities identify a target timing for the verification – target should be within 90 days of assuming position.
  5. Ensure Risk Approval Authority is delegated until verification of competency has been completed.

1.4 Candidate’s Role

  1. Complete the Process Safety Modules utilizing the LOFT Task book and this Assessor Guide.
  2. Regularly inform supervisor of progress.
  3. Mark completion on Career Connect.
  4. Liaise with Supervisor and Assessment team when ready for verification interview.

1.5 Verification and Assessment Process

  1. Assessors will ask open-ended questions to verify competency for each of the competencies/modules. Candidates should be prepared to elaborate and provide details and examples to demonstrate competency. Assessments should not be simply question and answer. Assessors should consider the candidates ability to speak fluidly about the concepts and principles that underpin the question when evaluating if competency has been demonstrated.
  2. Where a candidate requires further knowledge or skill development to demonstrate competency for a specific module(s) then a gap closure plan must be put in place to enable them to meet the competency requirement. All gaps must be re-assessed in each module before the overall competency is demonstrated. A continued delegation of Risk Approval Authority must be put in place until that competency can be demonstrated. Any areas where revisiting is required will be communicated at the session and follow-up actions identified.

1.6 Assessment Team

  1. In order to ensure consistency, the Business Unit should have a nominated LOFT Assessment team. It is expected that the assessment team should consist of at least three but no more than five people. Personnel involved in the assessment process will vary by site and the position of the candidate being assessed, but team composition should include the following roles.
  2. Assessment Lead – A BU experienced fourth-line Supervisor with Operations experience (Site Manager, or equivalent) should be the Assessment Lead to assess of a third Line Supervisor. A BU-experienced third-line Supervisor (Operations Manager or equivalent) should be the Assessment Lead of a second-line Supervisor. The role of this person is to lead the assessment and make the final decision as to whether the candidate has demonstrated the competency required.
  3. Assessment Facilitator – The role of this person is to coordinate and participate in the assessment, ensure the process is conducted fairly and professionally, and coordinate timing and logistics. Examples of an Assessment Facilitator could be Training and CAS Supervisors, SHE or OIMS Supervisors or other personnel experienced in conducting assessments.
  4. Technical Assessors – For each module it is important at least one person has the required technical competency and knowledge in the respective area to effectively assess the candidate’s competency. A Technical Assessor is someone with particular knowledge or experience in the asset or facility and who has specific technical knowledge of the processes being assessed. Consideration should be given to the knowledge and experience of the Assessment Lead and the Assessment Facilitator when selecting the Technical Assessor(s). Examples of these Technical Assessors could include but are not limited to: Technical Leads/Advisors for Asset/Site Process Safety Hazards/safeguards, Asset/Site Process Safety Technical Lead, Experienced Second and Third Line Supervisors, Operations First-Line Supervisors, Operations Advisors, Engineering Advisors, OIMS Element SME’s.
  5. Supervisor/Manager – It is permissible for the Supervisor or Manager of the candidate to be part of the Assessment process or act as a Technical/Module SME with the approval of the Assessment Lead. There may be situations where the appropriate SME is someone who reports directly to the LOFT candidate. This is also permissible with the approval of the Assessment Lead.

This Leadership Assessor Guide applies to all LOFT Process Safety Positions in Upstream Oil and Gas.

Describe the intent of the Risk Assessment and Management (RAM) Process
  • Provide a common framework for identifying and managing risks to people, environment, assets, and reputation.
  • Reduce identified risks to an acceptable level.
  • Prevent or mitigate the undesirable effects of potential events throughout the lifecycle of a facility, from early development through the production phase to life end.

What does ALARP stand for, and what is it meant to accomplish?
  • As Low As is Reasonably Practicable
  • ALARP - A risk acceptance review process that considers the best use of resources compared to the degree of risk reduction by asking the question "What else can be done to further reduce the risk?" ALARP is the point at which the resources expended to further reduce the risk are significantly disproportionate to the reduction in risk.
  • Risk can never be eliminated entirely, though it can be reduced through the application of hierarchy of safeguards. These safeguards prevent the undesired consequences. The ALARP principle arises from the fact that time, effort, and money cannot be spent on the attempt to reduce a risk to zero.
  • Risks should be managed to an acceptable level, subject to appropriate management review and approval. The determination of an acceptable level of risk is made by reviewing the safeguards against recognized and generally accepted standards and practices. Risk acceptance is a judgment call by the Risk Manager.
  • For HC1 and HC2 risks, the requirements established in the ExxonMobil ALARP Process for HC Risks shall be followed.
What is residual risk? Why review residual risks? How often should Residual Risk be reviewed?
  • Residual risk - the level of risk that remains after a risk scenario has been closed and accepted.
  • Residual risk is important since, even though reduced to an acceptable level, it has not been eliminated and remains a part of our operations.
  • With no active risk reduction measures being worked, there is potential to lose sight of these risks if not managed appropriately. Also, additional risk reduction options might be available or desirable that were not available at the time of closure.
  • Risks initially identified as Cat 1 and 2 are subject to the residual risk review process
  • Risks accepted and closed as Cat 1 are reviewed and approved annually.
  • Risks accepted and closed as Cat 2 or lower should be reviewed at least every 5 years, typically as part of the HAZOP/PHA revalidation process.

For additional information see Enterprise Risk Standard section 2.2.4 Residual Risk Review

Describe the process for approving a Risk Assessment - understanding the accepted risk and ensuring effective mitigation/appropriate timing
  • After completing the risk assessment, the risk assessment team reviews the results with the Risk Manager. In decision analysis, the Risk Manager does the following:
  • Consider the significance of the risk under study, including its effect to the aggregate risk of the asset
  • Evaluate the effectiveness and feasibility of the potential risk reduction measures.
  • Decide the course of further action to reduce the risk to an acceptable level (ALARP).
  • This activity requires considerable judgment, since the risk reduction benefits, and cost effectiveness of some potential risk reduction measures may be difficult to quantify.
  • The Risk Manager must also evaluate potential benefits of risk reduction measures against potential implementation risks.
When a critical safeguard associated with HC scenario is discovered to not meet performance criteria what action must be taken?
  • Safeguard owner informs all affected, including the scenario owner as well as operations, maintenance, and technical teams.
  • Scenario owner and site leadership take immediate action to determine if operations are safe to continue, identify any interconnected safeguards to be managed, and secure approval from the BU leadership team for continued operation.
  • Site teams restore the safeguard as a high priority through the “Break-in Work” process.

For additional information see Enterprise Risk Standard section 2.5.1 Critical Safeguards Management

    CVPE (Competency Verification Procedure Excellence) – Implementation 2025

    CVPE project involves the implementation of the Training and Competency Verification Upstream Best Practice (UBP) to ensure compliance with corporate standards and maintain operational integrity. The UBP outlines a structured approach incorporating human performance principles, defining competency expectations, and specific training outcomes for ExxonMobil Upstream operated facilities. It includes training and verifying the competency of Operations and Maintenance (O&M) personnel in key positions, maintaining collective competency, and ensuring operations integrity. Key positions are identified based on their impact on operations integrity, and training plans are developed to meet competency requirements through Foundational Craft Skill Standards (FCSS), Global Competencies, Unit Defined Competencies, and Facility Specific Training (FST). The project emphasizes task analysis to identify training needs, verification programs to ensure competency, and collective competency reviews to ensure adequate staffing. Verifiers play a crucial role in maintaining the quality and integrity of the verification process. The project also includes a mitigation process for managing personnel changes and addressing competency gaps, ensuring that all personnel in key positions are adequately trained and verified to perform their roles safely and effectively.

    Describe the objective of the Training and Competency UBP. Competency expectations are required to be maintained per key position — how are key positions defined?

    This is a structured and disciplined approach incorporating human performance principles, defining competency expectations, and defining specific training outcomes for ExxonMobil Upstream operated facilities. This UBP provides direction on how to:

    • Train, and verify competency of, field-based Operations and Maintenance (O&M) personnel holding Key Positions
    • Maintain collective competency and ensure operations integrity
    • A key position is one that could have a significant impact on operations integrity (OI) through individual decision-making authority, operational control, or individual actions, including emergency response. Section 1.2
