2UPBP-410  Q22.1 Risk Management

Identify any action items that are related to your higher consequence potential scenarios? Who is responsible for those action items and are there any overdue? If they are not overdue are they on track to be completed by the due date?

Answer as it currently stands in the study aid

Contact the Process Safety Team to obtain a copy of the latest KPI Scorecard and identify any actions associated with critical safeguards for each scenario.

Below is an overview of the monthly process safety scenario management KPI stewardship process.

  • Each scenario is assigned an Owner (Operations Manager or Drilling Operations Superintendent). Within each scenario, each critical safeguard has an assigned owner. Each safeguard in turn has key verification measures associated with it that are tracked monthly as KPIs to ensure critical safeguard health. Verification measures for a critical safeguard may be assigned to a role or extracted automatically from a database (e.g. SAP, ICSS, DCDA, WebCAT, etc.). KPIs are reviewed by Facility Integrity Management System (FIMS) / Safety Critical Element (SCE) owners and process safety SMEs before they are published.
  • KPIs not meeting the performance standard are noted on the KPI report and is presented monthly to the Asset Team onshore and offshore, and quarterly to the Operations Integrity Committee (OIC).
  • Findings and corrective actions for a KPI in a given month are noted on the KPI Scorecard, along with a target closure date.
  • Contact the Hibernia / Hebron SSHE Technician and / or Risk & Certification Engineer for a listing of current actions related to HC1 and HC2 risks.

    13UPBP-410  Q132.4 Critical Equipment

    Demonstrate an understanding of the designated Critical devices (e.g. gas detectors, shutdown valves) that are associated with critical safeguards. What extra controls are put in place when these devices are bypassed (if any)?

    Answer as it currently stands in the study aid
    • Below is a short list of typical critical safeguards. In general, all Safety Critical Devices have assigned Safety Critical Element (SCE) Performance Standard Criteria which dictate performance requirements, and trigger Incident Reporting within the OIMS 9-1 process if degraded or impaired. This may also trigger Certifying Authority and Regulatory Agency notification.
    • PSVs – Where redundant PSVs are installed in parallel, one of the two can be isolated. Cannot operate with both PSVs out of service (isolated or with failed EHT in winter months) without formal Risk Assessment approval.
    • PSTs – Failure will cause a PSD. Cannot operate with these out of service (defeated) without formal Risk Assessment approval.
    • BDVs/SDVs – Cannot operate with these out of service, or out of compliance with API 521 blowdown rates without formal Risk Assessment approval.
    • Fire & Gas Devices – For devices identified in the Critical Safeguard Performance Standards, no 2 devices of the same type (fire or gas) can be out of service in the same Fire Zone for more than 24 hours without a Risk Screening to continue operation.
    • BOP – Requires compliance with established SCE Performance Standard Impairment Criteria. Formal Risk Assessment required if system is partially impaired but can perform the minimum critical function as defined in the standard. Otherwise drilling shutdown is required.

    15UPBP-410  Q152.5 Alarm Management

    Indicate the actions required if you have an excursion outside your operating limits?

    Answer as it currently stands in the study aid
    • If an Operator identifies an excursion of an operating parameter outside of its normal operating limits (between its high and low set points) through alarm indication or rounds in the Central Control Room (CCR), they should take the following actions:
      • Investigate the cause of the alarm and assess its criticality
      • Assess the current process value versus the safety shutdown value (HH/LL) and take appropriate action to understand and mitigate the issue
      • Confirm that there are no safety critical devices (i.e. PSTs, TST) that have been defeated. If there is, remove the defeats to allow executive actions to take place as needed
      • In no circumstance should the Operator defeat a safety device to prevent a process shutdown
      • Communicate alarm and request field operator to investigate as needed

    Scenario Question: Breaking containment work is required to remove a faulty valve in hydrocarbon service. There is only a single valve isolation available to isolate and remove this valve. Describe the process approvals required to allow this work to progress.

    This question carries two answers: the Global Module answer (verifiable, from the Assessor Guide) and a Hebron/Hibernia answer (not verifiable here). Only the asset-local half needs checking.

    Answer as it currently stands in the study aid

    Global Module (Assessor Guide Rev 3.0):

    • Isolation valve leak and integrity test must be performed.
    • Isolation plan and use of an SVI for isolation must be approved by both the Production AA and the PIC.
    • If the flange class of the system is equal to or >300, the Operations AA or competent delegate (independent verifier to the AO) must be at the site to witness the demonstration of zero energy.
    • If the flange class is <300, the Operations AO must be at the site to witness the demonstration of zero energy — it cannot be delegated to the permit holder.

    Asset specific — Hebron/Hibernia:

  • If the single valve isolation is identified on the approved Appendix A Single Valve Isolation list, the applicable requirements and controls are followed.
  • If the SVI is not covered by the approved Appendix A list, a Risk Screening is completed. The Risk Screening determines whether a formal Risk Assessment is also required and identifies the necessary safeguards and controls. Professional Engineer review and approved stamp are required.
  • The isolation valve must provide a reliable seal and its integrity must be confirmed by an acceptable leak test or approved flange-breaking method. Zero energy must be demonstrated before breaking containment. If zero energy cannot be demonstrated, work must stop and the issue must be reassessed before proceeding.
  • An isolation valve leak and integrity test must be performed.
  • The isolation plan must be approved by both the Production AA and the Person in Charge (PIC). Permit Vision includes a function that requires the OIM to sign the permit if a single valve is used.
  • The AA must be present during the first break when using a single valve.
