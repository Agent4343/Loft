# Queries on the Process Safety Global Module Assessor's Guide Rev 3.0

Two wording issues found while building the Hebron/Hibernia study aid from
`UPBP-400-EN-01-RP-GBL Rev 3.0`. Neither is a defect in the study aid — it follows the
Assessor Guide as written in both cases — but both are worth a decision by the document
owner, because the study aid and every candidate assessed against it inherit whatever
the guide says.

Raised August 2026. No change is proposed to the study aid until the owner rules.

---

## 1. The guide says ten categories and then lists eleven

**Introduction and Preparation:**

> This Assessor Guide provides the questions and model answers across **ten** Process
> Safety categories.

**Process Safety Knowledge Requirements**, immediately after, lists:

| # | Category |
|---|---|
| 1 | Risk Management |
| 2 | **Competency Assurance Standard** |
| 3 | Training |
| 4 | Operating Procedures |
| 5 | Critical Equipment |
| 6 | Alarm Management |
| 7 | Work Management |
| 8 | Management of Change |
| 9 | Incident Investigation / Analysis |
| 10 | Environmental |
| 11 | Emergency Response |

That is eleven. The table of contents also runs to `11. Emergency Response`, and the
in-line numbering in that list restarts oddly — the printed digits read
`… Environmental 10 Emergency Response`, so the tenth label sits against the eleventh
entry.

**The likely explanation, for the owner to confirm.** The extra category is
**Competency Assurance Standard**, and the guide elsewhere gates the CAS questions on
whether a work area has completed CVPE:

> The following section only applicable if Your Work Area has not completed CVPE and is
> still using the CAS standard … or Skip to Chapter 3.

So "ten" is plausibly the count *after* CAS retires, written into an edition where the
CAS section is still present. If that is right, the fix is a note saying so, not a
recount.

**Why it matters.** A candidate who counts the categories to check their own coverage
gets a different number from the guide, and cannot tell which is authoritative. An
assessor building a checklist has the same problem.

**Suggested wording:** *"…across eleven Process Safety categories. Competency Assurance
Standard applies only to work areas that have not completed CVPE; where CVPE is
complete, ten categories are assessed."*

## 2. SLS and TLS are both told to inform the Site Manager directly

The two role descriptions under Risk Management end almost identically.

**Role of the SLS:**

> Immediately inform **Site Manager** of any critical safeguards no longer meeting
> performance criteria.

**Role of the TLS (Operations Manager):**

> Immediately inform **Site Manager** of any critical safeguards no longer meeting
> performance criteria and facilitate leadership team discussion on continued safe
> operations.

As written, the SLS escalates past the TLS to the Site Manager, and the TLS escalates to
the Site Manager as well. Everywhere else the guide describes a single-step ladder
(FLS → SLS → TLS → Site Manager).

**Two readings, and they lead to different answers in an interview:**

1. **Deliberate.** A critical safeguard below performance criteria is serious enough that
   the Site Manager is told immediately regardless of level, and the TLS's added job is
   the leadership discussion, not the notification. Under this reading the text is right
   and only reads oddly.
2. **A copy-paste from the TLS block into the SLS block.** Under this reading the SLS
   line should say *inform the TLS*.

**Why it matters.** This is an assessed question. A candidate asked to describe their
escalation route will be marked against whichever reading the assessor holds, and the
guide supports both. Reading 1 also has a practical consequence worth being deliberate
about: it puts the Site Manager on the direct receiving end of every SLS safeguard
notification across the asset.

**Requested:** confirm which reading is intended. If it is reading 1, a half-sentence
saying so ("this notification is direct at every level, by design") would remove the
ambiguity permanently.

---

## Handling in the study aid meanwhile

Both are carried **exactly as the guide words them**, under the standing rule that where
a study aid and the Assessor Guide disagree, the guide governs. A candidate is assessed
against what the assessor is reading, so a study aid that quietly "corrected" the
controlling document would set them up to give an answer the assessor marks wrong.

If the guide is revised, the two places to update are the category count in the
introduction section of the study aid and the SLS role answer under Risk Management.
