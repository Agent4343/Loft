1.0 Introduction and Preparation

1.1–1.6 Overview
This section is informational text as provided.
1.1 Introduction This Assessor Guide provides the questions and model answers across ten Process Safety categories for the Global Process Safety Modules. The answers to these questions should be the same for all Upstream Oil and Gas Business Units globally. It also contains the Asset Specific questions. It is an expectation that each Business Unit develop their own Asset Specific Assessor Guide from this template with the asset/facility specific answers to each of these questions. This guide should be utilized by both the candidate, in preparing for their face to face interview, and by the assessment teams, to ensure a consistency in assessment across all Business Units. This Guide is referred to as Appendix B under the LOFT Framework UBP.  This Combined Assessor Guide should be used for a specified position to conduct a complete assessment of Process Safety competency. This guide should be utilized by both the candidate, in preparing for their face to face interview, and by the assessment teams, to ensure a consistency in assessment across all Business Units. This Guide is referred to as Appendix B under the LOFT Framework UBP.  The assessment process can be undertaken once the candidate has completed their task book and is ready to demonstrate their competency. It is expected that this is conducted within 60 days of assuming the position. Risk Approval Authority for each position cannot be granted until the competency in the Process Safety Modules is demonstrated.  1.2 Purpose of Leadership Assessor Guide To provide a Global and Asset Specific assessment tool for new leaders to demonstrate competency in managing process safety and risk. Provide consistency in assessment of personnel in LOFT Process Safety positions across Upstream Oil and Gas. Provide a single point of resource material for Assessment Teams and Candidates.  1.3 Supervisor’s Role Review the Leadership Process Safety Competency Demonstration Assessor Guide / expectations with the candidate. Based on the individual’s previous assignments/work experiences assess the current “gap” for each section. Identify any extra developmental activities that would assist the candidate to improve their depth of understanding. Based on current gaps and recommended developmental activities identify a target timing for the verification – target should be within 60 days of assuming position. Ensure Risk Approval Authority is delegated until verification of competency has been completed.  1.4 Candidate’s Role Complete the Process Safety Modules utilizing the LOFT Task book and this Assessor Guide. Regularly inform supervisor of progress. Mark completion on Career Connect. Liaise with Supervisor and Assessment team when ready for verification interview.  1.5 Verification and Assessment Process Assessors will ask open-ended questions to verify competency for each of the competencies/modules. Candidates should be prepared to elaborate and provide details and examples to demonstrate competency. Assessments should not be simply question and answer. Assessors should consider the candidates ability to speak fluidly about the concepts and principles that underpin the question when evaluating if competency has been demonstrated.  Where a candidate requires further knowledge or skill development to demonstrate competency for a specific module(s) then a gap closure plan must be put in place to enable them to meet the competency requirement. All gaps must be re-assessed in each module before the overall competency is demonstrated. A continued delegation of Risk Approval Authority must be put in place until that competency can be demonstrated. Any areas where revisiting is required will be communicated at the session and follow-up actions identified.  1.6 Assessment Team [Exact text preserved as provided in your content.]

What does ALARP mean and what does it accomplish?
Definition and purpose
As Low As is Reasonably Practicable ALARP - A risk acceptance review process that considers the best use of resources compared to the degree of risk reduction by asking the question "What else can be done to further reduce the risk?" ALARP is the point at which the resources expended to further reduce the risk are significantly disproportionate to the reduction in risk. Risk can never be eliminated entirely, though it can be reduced through the application of hierarchy of safeguards. These safeguards prevent the undesired consequences. The ALARP principle arises from the fact that time, effort, and money cannot be spent on the attempt to reduce a risk to zero. Risks should be managed to an acceptable level, subject to appropriate management review and approval. The determination of an acceptable level of risk is made by reviewing the safeguards against recognized and generally accepted standards and practices. Risk acceptance is a judgment call by the Risk Manager. For HC1 and HC2 risks, the requirements established in the ExxonMobil ALARP Process for HC Risks shall be followed.
What is residual risk? Why review it? How often?
Definition, purpose, cadence
Residual risk - the level of risk that remains after a risk scenario has been closed and accepted. Residual risk is important since, even though reduced to an acceptable level, it has not been eliminated and remains a part of our operations. With no active risk reduction measures being worked, there is potential to lose sight of these risks if not managed appropriately. Also, additional risk reduction options might be available or desirable that were not available at the time of closure. Risks initially identified as Cat 1 and 2 are subject to the residual risk review process Risks accepted and closed as Cat 1 are reviewed and approved annually. Risks accepted and closed as Cat 2 or lower should be reviewed at least every 5 years, typically as part of the HAZOP/PHA revalidation process.  For additional information see Enterprise Risk Standard section 2.2.4 Residual Risk Review
Process for approving a Risk Assessment
Understanding accepted risk and mitigation/timing
After completing the risk assessment, the risk assessment team reviews the results with the Risk Manager. In decision analysis, the Risk Manager does the following: Consider the significance of the risk under study, including its effect to the aggregate risk of the asset Evaluate the effectiveness and feasibility of the potential risk reduction measures. Decide the course of further action to reduce the risk to an acceptable level (ALARP). This activity requires considerable judgment, since the risk reduction benefits, and cost effectiveness of some potential risk reduction measures may be difficult to quantify. The Risk Manager must also evaluate potential benefits of risk reduction measures against potential implementation risks.
Action when a critical safeguard (HC scenario) fails performance criteria
Immediate steps
Safeguard owner informs all affected, including the scenario owner as well as operations, maintenance, and technical teams. Scenario owner and site leadership take immediate action to determine if operations are safe to continue, identify any interconnected safeguards to be managed, and secure approval from the BU leadership team for continued operation. Site teams restore the safeguard as a high priority through the “Break-in Work” process.  For additional information see Enterprise Risk Standard section 2.5.1 Critical Safeguards Management
Scenario: piping below retirement thickness due to CUI
Options to manage risk
Ensure the following aspects are considered: Interim Mitigations – what mitigations can be put in immediately? Can the line be isolated or run at a lower pressure? Can a temporary repair be installed such as wrap or clamp? Does the area or piping need to be barricaded off? How are these mitigations communicated to all applicable personnel? Risk Screening – Initiate a risk screening to ensure the risk of keeping the like in service is being managed until repair. Ensure appropriate people are included in the risk screening and approve a triggered risk assessment if appropriate. Expedite Repair – evaluate options with Maintenance to expedite repair. Consider what other work can be re-prioritized to allow this to happen
